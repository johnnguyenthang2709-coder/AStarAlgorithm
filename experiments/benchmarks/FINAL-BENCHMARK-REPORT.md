# Final academic A* benchmark report

## 1. Research questions and scope

This frozen study addresses two separate questions. **Road:** on one fixed directed, distance-weighted road graph, does Haversine-guided C++ A* return the same minimum cost as Dijkstra, and how does measured search effort differ? **Robot:** at identical frozen positions, targets, and accumulated original-source sights, how do the authors' original approximate shortest path (ASP/DAP) routine and our continuous visibility-graph C++ A* differ in *planned local paths*? Supporting studies describe internal exploration policy, frontier-cache, sensing-radius, and observed-parent return behavior. They are not merged into either principal comparison.

The [paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.robot.2025.105185) proposes a bundle/DAP method for limited-range robot navigation. Our local comparison is **not** a reproduction of its published Section 6.2.1 numerical table or an intrinsic shortest-path comparison on one common graph. The authors' source skeleton/bundle representation and our scan-center visibility graph differ. The previously recorded [48-case end-to-end matrix](robot-authors-vs-astar.csv) remains **blocked** as a comparable physical-motion experiment for the reasons in the [independent motion audit](author-motion-validity-audit.md): the pinned source jumps between selected coordinates, while its computed ASP polyline is logged/drawn, and neither a continuous executed trajectory nor a common robot-footprint model is established. No blocked values are promoted to comparative results here.

## 3. Road A* versus Dijkstra

The [validator](validate_road.py) checks the pinned graph hash, every directed edge's Haversine lower-bound inequality, each pair's identity and common cost objective, all 180 cost matches to both Dijkstra and the frozen offline distance, and start=goal, one-way, unreachable, and edge-midpoint cases. The Haversine scale remains 1.0 and there are **zero** edge-consistency violations at the stated numerical tolerance. Both methods use the same directed graph and edge length in meters. The [raw 360 algorithm rows](road-astar-dijkstra.csv) and [validation JSON](road-validation.json) are the source of the table.

| Stratum (60 each) | Median A* / Dijkstra expansions | Median paired expansion reduction | Median A* / Dijkstra search µs | Median paired time ratio |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Short | 101 / 505 | 78.6% | 44.95 / 128.09 | 0.324 |
| Medium | 301 / 1,592 | 77.4% | 282.95 / 1,117.24 | 0.287 |
| Long | 714.5 / 2,588.5 | 68.6% | 771.46 / 1,883.19 | 0.449 |

All **180/180** optimal costs agree; A* expanded fewer valid nodes in **180/180**. A* had a lower search-call median in **173/180**, Dijkstra in **7/180**. Across all pairs, the median paired expansion reduction is **76.0%**, and the median paired search-time ratio is **0.338** (range **0.061–1.435**). Pair **167** has the largest A*/Dijkstra time ratio, 1.435. The paired reduction and ratio are computed **per pair before taking a median**; ratios of the displayed marginal medians are different statistics. These timings exclude road loading, snapping, API, and map rendering; they do not predict an end-to-end UI speedup.

## 4. Authors' source and eligibility audit

The authors' original `Robot_run.py` uses finite-range sensing, picks an open point, finds a skeleton, calls `approximately_shortest_path`, appends the resulting ASP to its path log, and then assigns the selected point as the next coordinate. The separate [motion-semantics audit](author-motion-validity-audit.md) reproduces five hypothetical straight inter-state links crossing polygon interiors across two episodes, while the corresponding planned ASP segments avoid interiors. It also documents boundary touches and radius-0.5 clearance failures. That is a source-state versus physical-motion distinction, not proof that the paper's mathematical DAP algorithm is invalid.

The [exhaustive eligibility CSV](robot-snapshot-eligibility-audit.csv) has **one row per 351 captured source decision**. The [summary](robot-snapshot-eligibility-summary.json) separately preserves the original **118 captured / 7 eligible / 111 excluded** audit:

| Mutually exclusive original-cohort reason | Decisions |
| --- | ---: |
| Two-vertex direct path, target visible under original sight predicate; no bundle refinement | 61 |
| Two-vertex direct path at the sensing-radius boundary; no bundle refinement | 50 |
| Nontrivial skeleton with more than two vertices; eligible | 7 |

For the 50 boundary cases, the source's strict `< radius` point predicate and our closed sight-boundary convention disagree on the endpoint. The source graph nevertheless contains its direct start–target edge; the recorded ASP is the same two-vertex segment, and the full segment is certified by frozen sights. This is a boundary convention nuance, **not** a discovered-space conversion failure. All 111 excluded direct links avoid original polygon interiors and boundaries in an independent offline check. They could support a **trivial direct-path comparison**, but the preregistered research question specifically selects decisions that invoke the authors' bundle refinement; adding them would dilute that question. There were **zero** missing-data, endpoint, geometry-information, or eligibility-checker mismatches. Eligibility uses the original skeleton vertex count and input completeness, never which planner wins or whether one planner later fails. Every independently eligible state was retained.

Across all three frozen cohorts, **351** decisions yielded **21** eligible, **330** excluded direct cases. The new extension contributed **233/14/219** respectively. Across all 351, the mutually exclusive direct categories are **176** source-visible and **154** at strict range boundary. The retained 21 cases span five source map files; maps and decisions are not independent random experimental units, and the original/held-out bugtrap states overlap geometrically.

## 5. Robot local planned-path methods and geometric validity

Each frozen decision contains the robot coordinate, selected target, accumulated `visited_sights` (centers, blocked fragments, open fragments), source graph/skeleton, ASP vertices, source revision, and map hash. The authors' unchanged `approximately_shortest_path` is replayed from this state and its vertices must match the recorded source ASP. Our unchanged C++ `visibility_search` runs on scan-center vertices plus the same endpoints; proposed graph links are certified over their **entire segment** inside the union of recorded free sights. The planners do **not** receive original full obstacle polygons. The polygons enter only after plans are written for independent offline validity checks and diagnostic figures. Both methods have the same frozen observed information and endpoints, but **different graph constructions**. The A* path is shortest on its constructed graph; no claim of globally shortest continuous path in unknown space follows.

Primary validity is a **point-center, obstacle-interior-only** convention: correct endpoints within `1e-7`, complete segment coverage by the accumulated observed sight model, and no entry into original polygon interiors. Obstacle-boundary contact is allowed. The separately reported **strict point** convention also rejects boundary contact. The source's nominal `robot_radius=0.5` is a simulator parameter here; path centerline clearance is reported only as a sensitivity diagnostic. The benchmark does **not** validate disk-robot motion in configuration space. Euclidean planned path length is the sum of its segments; a turn is a nonzero heading change greater than `1e-6` radians, and the report's interpretable significant-turn view uses >15°. Small ASP zigzags can inflate the all-turn count.

The [per-pair CSV](robot-local-final-paired.csv), three cohort [geometry CSVs](robot-local-benchmark/robot-local-geometry-validation.csv), [held-out geometry](robot-local-extension/robot-local-geometry-validation.csv), and [prospective geometry](robot-local-final-extension/robot-local-geometry-validation.csv) retain path lengths, turns, endpoints, boundary contacts, minimum clearance, sight certification, and offline polygon validity. The geometry adapter checks accepted A* links continuously through analytic event splitting under the recorded circle/straight-blocker model. For the new 233 decisions, the source-versus-derived membership sample had **0/116,500** disagreements, and **0/98,703** sampled points on accepted graph links were uncovered by the source predicate. All **518** recorded source ASP segments were sight-certified, and all **997** accepted A* links avoided original polygon interiors and boundaries offline. These sampled source-predicate agreements supplement, rather than prove universal equivalence to every possible sensing implementation.

Two new cases have equal planned lengths. Both planners found all **21/21** eligible local paths, with correct endpoints and primary interior-only validity. There are no eligible local planning failures in this sample. The mean paired A*−ASP length is **+2.981** world units; the median is **0.000**, range **−10.707 to +44.266**. The largest positive difference is `P02-_map_deadend-01`: ASP length 15.734 versus A* 60.000, but ASP touches an obstacle boundary and is **not strictly valid**. All six ASP boundary-touch cases are shorter under the permissive primary convention; among the **15** pairs where both routes are strictly valid, ASP is shorter in **3**, A* in **10**, and **2** are equal (median paired delta **−2.584**). This does **not** mean A* intrinsically dominates ASP: sparse graph representation and point-boundary convention materially affect feasible paths. Significant-turn medians are ASP **2** and A* **1**, with median paired all-turn delta **−2**; the latter is sensitive to ASP's small polyline bends.

### Runtime component boundaries

The median of per-state medians across 21 states is **62.655 ms** for the original Python ASP core call, comprising recorded critical-line construction median **0.319 ms** and optimization median **62.400 ms** as source-returned components. Our sight-certified graph construction median is **42.102 ms**, while the C++ binding/search median is **0.0263 ms**; the median per-state sum of those two A* components is **42.142 ms**. [Raw 31-call samples](robot-local-final-extension/authors-asp-timing.json) and [A* samples](robot-local-final-extension/astar-plans.json), along with each cohort's timing-distribution CSV, expose variability. The source skeleton build, online sensing, and exploration are outside the ASP core timing. Our observed-space conversion and graph certification are included in A* build time, not its search-call time. Different graph representations and Python/C++ implementations prevent treating the isolated 0.0263-ms call as a head-to-head speedup or treating the above sums as completely symmetric end-to-end pipelines. A* expanded-node counts and ASP critical-line counts are method-specific effort indicators, not a common operation count.

## 6. Supporting online-navigation checks

The [supporting validator](validate_supporting.py) checks the original paired keys, successes, statuses, cache equivalence, and radius configurations. In [20 legacy/radar pairs](exploration-ablation.csv), both reached their goals and radar traveled less in **18**, more in **2**; the median paired radar-minus-legacy distance is **−95.419** world units. The largest regression, **+70.772**, is Complex Irregular Labyrinth radius 7. Therefore the radar policy is retained as a useful but non-dominating engineering choice. In [20 full/cached frontier pairs](frontier-cache.csv), playback and recovery outcomes are equivalent **20/20**; median frontier processing is **639.166 ms** full versus **46.723 ms** cached. In the [12 sensor-radius episodes](sensor-robustness.csv), **12/12** reached the goal; radius 5/7 distance medians are **92.909/113.278** world units. This small set does not establish monotonic distance improvement with range.

The [indoor parent-return trace](../../docs/bar-indoor-radar-trace.csv) contains four observed, certified, nonfallback return events used in the [representative figure](final-figures/support-parent-return.svg). Its labels refer to observed branch/parent state, not hidden room topology. Conditional return-length certification compares the actual executed geometric return with the recorded reversible entry between the **same endpoints**. C++ A* plans on discovered visibility links; reversed entry remains a verified fallback. This is a conditional BAR bound under recorded collision-free reversible entry and matching endpoints, not a global unknown-world navigation guarantee. The trace and earlier [parent-backtracking audit](../../docs/bar-parent-backtracking-audit.md) contain the detailed triggers and regression cases.

## 7. Threats to validity and conclusion

The robot sample comprises 21 nonrandom, correlated local decisions from only five map files; 330 of 351 captured decisions were direct cases outside the bundle-refinement question. The initial and prior held-out bugtrap decisions are near duplicates. The prospective extension added distinct dead-end, blocks, and irregular-map states, but its source episodes share maps and sensing rules. One episode hit its iteration cap. Geometry uses an ideal point center; six ASP routes touch boundaries and finite-radius safety is unproven even for some apparently clear centerlines. The authors' online source motion and our collision-checked online controller have incompatible execution semantics, so no end-to-end superiority or paper-table reproduction is claimed. Runtime components have different preparation boundaries and languages. The road result is strong **for this fixed graph and frozen pair set**, not a universal timing theorem.

Within those limits, the road experiment verifies equal optimal costs and consistently lower A* search effort on its fixed directed graph. The robot experiment is a transparent, fair, frozen-state comparison of two different local planning representations using the same observed sights and endpoints. Its mixed path-length results and collision-convention sensitivity are appropriate descriptive evidence for a university report. The end-to-end validity gate remains intact.
